## Likely questions and answers

## 1) Tell me about yourself.

### Good answer
"I’ve spent the last 13+ years in full-cycle consultative sales, primarily in homebuilding and real estate-adjacent environments. My work has involved managing long-cycle deals, coordinating multiple stakeholders, maintaining disciplined pipelines, and building relationships with agents and partners that drove repeat and referral business. I’ve also owned and operated a franchise, so I understand the operator side of buying decisions too. What stood out to me about Luxury Presence is that the customer base is one I already understand really well, but the product lets me move from selling the end transaction to selling the platform that helps agents grow their business."

## 2) Why Luxury Presence?

### Good answer
"Because it’s one of the few roles where my background lines up naturally with the buyer. I already understand how agents think about growth, brand, referral business, and the pressure to stand out in a crowded market. So this doesn’t feel like forcing a random software story — it feels like a very believable move into a platform that serves a customer world I already know well."

## 3) Why move from home sales into software?

### Good answer
"The part that attracts me is not just ‘software’ in the abstract. It’s that I’d still be in a consultative, relationship-driven sales motion, but now selling a platform that helps agents grow and operate better. I already understand that customer pain. So to me it feels less like changing identities and more like moving closer to the tools side of a market I already know well."

## 4) Do you have SaaS experience?

### Good answer
"Not with the exact title in a pure SaaS org, no. But I do have the core commercial motion: discovery, qualification, multi-stakeholder selling, CRM discipline, negotiation, and close. What makes me unusually relevant here is that I also understand the actual customer Luxury Presence sells to. So while the product category is different, the selling work and the buyer context are not foreign to me."

## 5) How do you manage a pipeline?

### Good answer
"I like to keep pipeline management simple, disciplined, and honest. I want clean next steps, clear stage definitions, and a realistic read on where deals actually are. I’ve worked in Salesforce, HubSpot, and Dynamics 365, and I’m used to managing a lot of concurrent opportunities without losing visibility into timing, risk, or stakeholder movement."

## 6) How do you sell consultatively?

### Good answer
"For me it starts with understanding how the customer actually wins. I want to understand their business model, where they feel pressure, how they currently operate, and what they think is limiting growth. Once that’s clear, the conversation becomes less about features and more about whether the solution actually helps them move the business forward."

## 7) Tell me about working with multiple stakeholders.

### Good answer
"That’s been normal for me for years. In home sales, I’ve had to keep buyers, agents, lenders, title, construction, and internal teams aligned at the same time. Different priorities, different personalities, different timing pressure. So I’m very used to keeping momentum, clarifying next steps, and making sure nothing stalls because communication got sloppy."

## Good questions to ask them

Pick 3–5, don’t machine-gun all of them.

- What separates the AEs who ramp fastest here from the ones who struggle?
- What does the first 90 days look like in practice?
- How much of the motion is truly net-new vs inbound/opportunistic?
- Who are the strongest customer profiles right now — solo luxury agents, teams, brokerages?
- What are the most common reasons deals stall or get lost?
- How do your top reps create urgency when agents already have some kind of website/branding setup?
- What does a great handoff to onboarding/customer success look like?

## Objections / concerns they may have

## Concern: no pure SaaS title pedigree

### Response
Don’t get defensive.
Acknowledge it and redirect to:
- same commercial fundamentals
- stronger customer-world understanding
- faster buyer empathy than random SaaS outsider

## Concern: transition from home sales to software

### Response
Frame it as evolution, not escape:
- still consultative
- still trust-driven
- still growth/ROI based
- now selling the system instead of the end transaction

## Concern: can he ramp on product/tech fast enough?

### Response
Use the AI/operator angle lightly:
- already comfortable learning tools quickly
- already uses AI and automation in real workflows
- not an engineer, but not intimidated by technical products either
